# Ghost and environment remodel verification

The approved nonbloody ghost concept informs the new face, pale skin, worn offwhite dress and wet hair. This is a real-time procedural 3D adaptation, not a photogrammetry model or a one-to-one recreation of the concept image.

## Changes

- Three original raster sources: face, character materials and environment materials. Shared physical surface palette covers all playable areas and the road sequence.
- Continuous deforming arms, scalp-anchored alpha hair with gravity and floor clearance, independent recessed mouth and jaw, refined hands/feet and cloth silhouette.
- Modeled inset sinks, supported faucets, a rectangular bath, ceramic wet-room surfaces, cabinet construction and stove grates.
- Sloped sedan windows, wheel arches, mirrors and door details; round pipes/rails; mounted call panel and door hardware; CRT casing, buttons and cable; road windows constrained to building height.
- Subtle room reflections, bevels and material-scale grain. Removed duplicate kitchen plumbing/dressing after replacement.

## Verification

- Full suite: 96 passing (story, full input-driven game route, captures, stamina, saves, geometry and placements).
- Ghost suite rerun after the final cloth fit: 10 passing, including stepped-floor vertex clearance, seated ending, deterministic pause and attack framing.
- New ray tests cover the mounted elevator call plate and closed sink corners/recessed interior.
- TypeScript check and production build successful.
- Actual Three.js renders inspected for apartment entry/kitchen/bathroom, landing, lift, corridor, stairs, 1704, management office, parking and road; ghost standing/side/crawling/attack also inspected.
- Desktop Chrome road render: median 16.7 ms, p95 18.2 ms, 37 visible draw calls. This is a local sample, not a cross-device performance guarantee.
- Read-only review findings resolved: sink/burner overlap, flange gaps, basin winding, tub support, shower brackets and door-handle mounting.

Visuals remain procedural real-time assets. Fine silhouette and facial deformation are still less detailed than the photographic concept, particularly in bright inspection lighting.
